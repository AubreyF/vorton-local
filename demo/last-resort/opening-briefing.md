# Opening weekend: keep it small

This is an authored fictional demonstration. These are invented staff perspectives, not independent agent runs or quotations from real people.

## Mabel Meridian

We can make twelve guests feel welcome. We cannot make a missing corridor feel intentional. Inspect the east wing, rehearse one arrival, and give each guest a clear way home. I would like to open bookings this week, provided Penny can bound the refund promise. Hospitality begins with a promise we can keep.

## Ivo Null

Gravity in rooms 7 through 12 needs a measured test. Room 404 remains blocked until the survey finds a door; the booking ledger is not a substitute for architecture. Test with a tethered suitcase before a guest. The universe has declined our request for a staging environment, so we will use an empty room.

## Solstice Bell

A twelve-guest rehearsal is useful if reception has cover. I can welcome a late arrival or negotiate with a backward-ringing bell, but doing both at once requires another person. Enlightenment has not changed the rota. Record every request that needs a second pair of hands and review it before taking paid bookings.

## Penny Perihelion

Defer brochure spending until the wing passes inspection. Sell a bed, breakfast, and a view. Do not sell transcendence with an unlimited refund clause. I support an internal rehearsal with no guest deposits; our fictional budget is finite even when the marketing copy is not.

## Disagreement

Mabel wants to open bookings this week. Penny wants evidence from the rehearsal first. Solstice requires shift coverage, and Ivo requires completed room checks. The recommended next step is an internal rehearsal after inspections, with paid bookings deferred. That is a proposal awaiting owner acceptance.

## Alternatives

An internal rehearsal gives the staff a bounded test without taking deposits. A full reopening before inspections is rejected. Closing the hotel indefinitely remains unnecessary while the small rehearsal is feasible.

## Evidence

All evidence is fictional: the east-wing inspection checklist, the Room 404 corridor survey work order, and the kitchen rehearsal notes in this workspace. No real maintenance inspection, guest booking, or financial transaction occurred.
